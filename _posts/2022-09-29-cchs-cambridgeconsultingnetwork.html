Around 1500+ children and young adults are affected by Congenital Central Hypoventilation Syndrome (CCHS). CCHS children are at extreme risk of death or permanent disablement when sleeping.

The most recognised symptom of CCHS is the inability to control breathing that varies in severity, resulting in the need for life-long mechanical ventilatory support during sleep in some patients or all the time in others.

Currently the only way to ensure via a ventilator, usually via a tracheostomy. There are existing diaphragm pacers available in the United States and come parts of Europe however it requires children to be permanently ‘connected’ via leads to a backpack housing an external signaller and does not remove the requirement for ventilation via a ventilator when asleep. We want to bring life changing treatments to fruition for these children.

Experts have identified a small number of opportunities to dramatically improve the quality of life for these children.
The Cambridge University Consulting Network has been tasked to investigate the timescale to achieve the following two treatments, both mechanical in their nature:

1) Development of a “totally implantable biofeedback diaphragm pacer system that will be capable of sensing the patient’s ventilatory needs and automatically adjust ventilatory demands of the patient. This in simple terms would act in a similar way to a cardiac pacemaker that we are all familiar with. The rate limiting steps include signalling pathways and battery longevity

2) Development of a closed loop nerve stimulation system capable via an algorithm of automatic adjustment of breathing rate and potentially depth while asleep. Either part invasive or non invasive. Successful development of this would enable children with CCHS and other respiratory diseases or issues to sleep without being connected to life support based ventilators.
