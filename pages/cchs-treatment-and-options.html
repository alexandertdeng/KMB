Introduction to CCHS Treatment

  • We have previously covered the advantages and disadvantages of different treatments for CCHS in this article.
  • Currently if a patient is born and diagnosed with CCHS in early infancy (within the first few months of life) there will be two primary options for treatment to enable your child (the patient) to come home safely. These are via supported ventilation by one of two methods.
  • 1. Tracheostomy
  • 2. Mask Ventilation
  • Parents, and depending on the country, a team of nurses or carers to deliver the treatment is essential to bringing the child back home in as fast a timeline as it safely possible. Depending on diagnosis, other symptoms, clinicians, hospital and even country, this can be as soon as weeks, or many, many months.
  • Each method of treatment (both artificial ventilation) has advantages and disadvantages and it is very important to consider both the medical and quality of life implications of each choice. For help with this, please read this article.

Current available treatments for CCHS

Tracheostomy - A basic explanation

A tracheostomy with mechanical ventilation is an intervention for newborns or infants. It is a surgical procedure that involves creating an opening in the trachea to insert a tube, providing direct airway access to ensure adequate ventilation.

Mask Ventilation - A basic explanation

Non-invasive mask ventilation is an alternative treatment for newborns and infants with CCHS. This approach uses a mask or nasal interface to deliver positive airway pressure, supporting the child’s breathing.
