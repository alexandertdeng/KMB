How many people have CCHS?

Nobody yet knows exactly. Only four published studies have reported an original birth prevalence estimate. Recent registry data put it at 1 in 67,400 births in South Korea and 1 in 105,334 in France, higher than older estimates, with Japan and Israel in a similar range.

Based on these figures, a forthcoming analysis by Rand et al. estimates that 11,733 to 39,602 people aged 21 or under are living with CCHS worldwide, far more than registries currently count. The true number may be higher still: diagnosis needs genetic testing, and some infants with undiagnosed CCHS die suddenly (SIDS).

This condition varies in severity, and is linked with other conditions such as Neuroblastoma (a type of childhood cancer) and Hirschsprung’s disease.

Sometimes in very mild CCHS cases, CCHS itself can be missed, but other symptoms can still appear (Neuroblastoma being an example), with CCHS only being diagnosed upon the commencement of treatment for other symptoms, or not at all.

CCHS and SIDS (Sudden Infant Death Syndrome)

Some genetic variants in the gene PHOX2B have been found in studies of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS)

However it has not been established whether these genetic variants are causally linked to sudden infant death syndrome, or whether they are incidental findings that are part of normal human genetic variation. This is something we strongly advocate exploring.

The Evidence: How we know CCHS and SIDS are linked

Multiple PHOX2B mutations have been identified via autopsy and post genetic testing in Stillborn and SIDS cases. See a paper published in November 2025 for further evidence here: Hirschsprung Disease on Fetal Autopsy Leading to the Diagnosis of Congenital Central Hypoventilation Syndrome in a Stillborn Fetus

Up to 1 in 10 sudden infant deaths may involve genetic variants in the PHOX2B gene, the same gene responsible for CCHS. (Two studies, showing 4% and 13% of SIDS cases had some kind of deviation in PHOX2B (contraction, polymorphism) another with expansion. CCHS Cases by Country

Official figures are hard to come by

Official numbers do not exist. In the UK there are estimated to be around 150 confirmed and diagnosed cases with hundreds more in the USA and across Europe. A significant cohort exist in Australia, Japan and other countries too.

Why primarily children?

CCHS cases are child heavy

In the past, many of these patients died in infancy and childhood: the 2005 French registry reported 38% mortality. More recent studies put it at about 9–14%, with around three in four deaths happening before the age of two. More babies with CCHS now survive, but the dangers and the impact on quality of life remain. We will change that.
